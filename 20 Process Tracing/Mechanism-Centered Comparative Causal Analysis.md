# Mechanism-Centered Comparative Causal Analysis

A useful label for the emerging general method.

1. Extract the causal claim.
2. Identify explanans, explanandum, and mechanism.
3. Operationalize concepts.
4. Separate constraints, incentives, capabilities, preferences, beliefs, and information.
5. Generate alternative mechanisms and actor responses.
6. Identify omitted variables and interactions.
7. State counterfactuals.
8. Seek hard, deviant, negative, and near-miss cases.
9. Identify temporal, technological, and institutional scope.
10. State what evidence would reduce confidence.
11. Test mechanisms within cases.
12. Compare across cases where generalization is intended.

Intellectual relatives: process tracing; structured, focused comparison; comparative-historical analysis; mechanism-based explanation; counterfactual analysis; structural causal modeling; Bayesian inference; systems analysis; conceptual analysis; critical rationalism.

Target: make theories behave increasingly like **generative models** rather than persuasive narratives.
